Photosynthesis is the process by which green plants make their own food using light energy. It takes place mainly in the chloroplasts of leaf cells, which contain the green pigment chlorophyll. The overall equation is 6CO2 + 6H2O + light energy -> C6H12O6 + 6O2. It happens in two stages. The light-dependent reactions occur in the thylakoid membranes: light splits water (photolysis), oxygen is released, and ATP and NADPH are produced. The light-independent reactions (Calvin cycle) occur in the stroma: carbon dioxide is fixed by the enzyme RuBisCO and, using ATP and NADPH, converted into glucose. Factors that affect the rate of photosynthesis include light intensity, carbon dioxide concentration and temperature.
