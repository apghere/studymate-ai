TCP (Transmission Control Protocol) is connection-oriented. It sets up a connection with a three-way handshake (SYN, SYN-ACK, ACK), guarantees reliable, in-order delivery using sequence numbers, acknowledgements and retransmission, and provides flow control (sliding window) and congestion control. Used by HTTP/HTTPS, email (SMTP) and file transfer (FTP).
UDP (User Datagram Protocol) is connectionless. No handshake, no guarantee of delivery or order, and a small 8-byte header compared with TCP's minimum 20 bytes. It is faster and has lower latency, so it is used for DNS queries, video streaming, online gaming and VoIP, where speed matters more than perfect reliability.
